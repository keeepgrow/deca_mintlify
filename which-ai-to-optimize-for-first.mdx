**AI now answers questions instead of pointing to links, and those answers are splitting across ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity. Few teams can optimize for all of them at once, so the practical question is which one to start with.**

---

> **Quick answer:** People increasingly get information from an AI answer, not a list of links. Six in ten US adults now read AI-generated search summaries ([Pew Research, June 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)). At the same time, use is spreading across more assistants, so "optimize for AI" is no longer a plan by itself. With limited time and budget, the best first move in 2026 is ChatGPT: it is still the single most-used engine (44% of US adults, about double the next tool) and it produces the most answers. This is the same order DecaGEO, an AI-visibility tracker, follows in what it measures.

## AI is answering, not linking

The reason AI visibility matters is that the answer has become the destination. Six in ten US adults say they read AI-generated search summaries ([Pew Research, June 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)). When someone gets what they need from the summary, the ten blue links below it never get a click.

That changes what "being found" means. It used to mean ranking a page. Now it means being named inside the answer the model writes. If your brand is not in that answer, you are invisible to the person reading it, no matter where your page sits in traditional search.

## And the answers are splitting across platforms

The catch is that there is no longer one place to be named. ChatGPT's share of the AI-assistant market slipped below 50% for the first time in 2026, closing May near 46% as Gemini, Claude, and Grok pulled users in ([Sensor Tower, reported June 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/16/chatgpts-market-share-slips-below-50-for-first-time/)). People now move between several assistants rather than living in one.

So "optimize for AI" stops being an instruction and becomes a question: optimize for *which* AI? A brand with unlimited resources would cover all of them at once. Almost no brand has that. The real decision is where to spend a first, limited round of effort so it actually shows up in results.

## With limited resources, start where the most people are

When you can only cover one engine well, four practical things decide which one: reach, answer volume, groundwork that transfers, and data you can trust. Weigh the engines against those four, and in 2026 the same one comes out ahead. The market has more players than it used to, but the one most people actually use hasn't changed.

| What a first engine needs | Why it matters | ChatGPT, 2026 |
|---|---|---|
| The widest reach | A representative signal, not a niche one | 44% of US adults use it, about double the next engine ([Pew, June 2026](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2026/06/17/americans-and-ai-2026-chatbots-smart-devices-and-views-on-impact/)) |
| The most answers produced | The biggest place to be seen | ~900M weekly users ([OpenAI, February 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/02/27/chatgpt-reaches-900m-weekly-active-users/)), and near 1B monthly app users ([Sensor Tower est., June 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/06/12/chatgpt-a-billion-monthly-app-users-despite-souring-public-ai-sentiment.html)) |
| Groundwork that transfers | A first round of effort isn't wasted | ~78% of AI citations go to brands' own sites, and the pattern holds across engines ([arXiv, June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20065)) |
| Data you can trust | Weekly numbers you can act on | The searches and keywords behind each answer are visible |

_Source: Pew Research (June 2026), OpenAI via TechCrunch (February 2026), Sensor Tower via CNBC (June 2026), Kumar (arXiv, June 2026)._

The third one is where the savings are. AI answers cite brands' own sites more than anything else, and that holds across assistants, so the pages you build to be found on ChatGPT carry most of the way to Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity. You build the foundation once, not again for each engine.

This comes up with customers a lot. They ask about the others early: can you do Perplexity, what about Gemini? It's a fair question. For a brand that's just getting started, though, there's usually a lot of foundational work to set up first, the pages and structure an engine needs before it can cite you at all. ChatGPT is the natural place to begin, and most of that setup carries over to the other engines when you're ready.

## What this means for your brand

If you are deciding where to spend a first round of AI-visibility effort, start where the answers are and start with your own pages. Make your pricing, feature, and comparison pages clean and extractable for ChatGPT, since roughly three-quarters of AI citations land on brands' own sites ([Kumar, arXiv, June 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20065)). Then watch whether your brand's recommendation rank actually moves, week over week. Broaden to Gemini, Claude, and Perplexity after the first engine is set up, not before.
